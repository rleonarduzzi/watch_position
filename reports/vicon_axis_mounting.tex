\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries IMU $x,y$ opposed to the optical body}
\end{center}
\smallskip

The optical body is the rigid body tracked by Vicon.
Its lab position is $\mathbf{p}$ (\texttt{gt\_pos}) and its attitude is the Hamilton quaternion $q$ (\texttt{gt\_rot\_quat}, stored scalar last), body to world:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{lab}} = R(q)\,\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{body}} .
  \label{eq:body}
\end{equation}
Body $z$ is up. Body $x$ and $y$ are the other two axes of that Vicon definition.
The IMU is a second frame on the same object.
On every sequence its $z$ axis matches the optical $z$.
On \texttt{v3\_02}, \texttt{v3\_12}, \texttt{v3\_14} and \texttt{v3\_15} its $x$ and $y$ axes point the opposite way, which is the mounting
\begin{equation}
  R_z(\pi) = \operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,+1),
  \qquad
  \mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{body}} = R_z(\pi)\,\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{imu}} .
  \label{eq:rz}
\end{equation}

\section{Tests}
Both tests use the first $120\,\mathrm{s}$ of each recording, at $\Delta t = 0.01\,\mathrm{s}$.

The optical body rate is the right-trivialised increment of $q$.
Over one sample the attitude advances by a body rotation,
\[
  q_{k+1} = q_k\otimes\exp(\boldsymbol{\omega}_k\Delta t),
\]
where $\exp$ sends the rotation vector $\boldsymbol{\omega}_k\Delta t$ to the corresponding unit quaternion.
Left-multiply by $q_k^{-1}$. Associativity and $q_k^{-1}\otimes q_k = 1$ isolate the increment,
\[
  q_k^{-1}\otimes q_{k+1} = \exp(\boldsymbol{\omega}_k\Delta t).
\]
$\mathrm{Log}$ inverts $\exp$ for a rotation of angle less than $\pi$, so
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\omega}_k
  = \frac{1}{\Delta t}\,\mathrm{Log}\bigl(q_k^{-1}\otimes q_{k+1}\bigr) .
  \label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
It is compared with the gyroscope midpoint
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_k = \tfrac12\bigl(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\mathrm{imu}}_k + \boldsymbol{\omega}^{\mathrm{imu}}_{k+1}\bigr)$
on samples with $\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\| > 0.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
With $c_i = \mathrm{corr}(\tilde\omega_i, \omega_i)$ the per-axis correlation, reverse the horizontal axes when the reversed signs agree better:
\begin{equation}
  \text{reverse } x,y
  \iff
  -c_x - c_y + c_z > c_x + c_y + c_z
  \iff
  c_x + c_y < 0 .
  \label{eq:test}
\end{equation}

On a sequence whose axes agree the two rates overlie.
On a reversed sequence they keep the same shape, with opposite sign on $x$ and $y$ and the same sign on $z$.
The stretches are a few seconds of large motion.

The second test does not use the gyroscope.
From $20\,\mathrm{s}$ to $110\,\mathrm{s}$, the lab specific force is a 9-sample moving average of
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{f}_k
  = \frac{\mathbf{p}_{k+1} - 2\mathbf{p}_k + \mathbf{p}_{k-1}}{\Delta t^2}
  + \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ g \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:force}
\end{equation}
and the accelerometer is rotated by the same quaternion under each mounting,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{lab}} = R(q)\,R\,\mathbf{a},
  \qquad R \in \{I,\, R_z(\pi)\} .
  \label{eq:acc}
\end{equation}
With $R = I$, the same two stretches give the accelerometer against the specific force.
On \texttt{v3\_13} the accelerometer follows $f$.
On \texttt{v3\_12} the traces run against each other, and the lab vertical of the accelerometer sits near $-g$ while $f_z$ sits near $+g$.
A pure sign flip of lab $x$ and $y$ is not what $R(q)$ does to a body yaw, so those two components are not mirrors of $f$.

\section{Correction}
When \eqref{eq:test} holds, the measurements are mapped into the optical body and the pose is left as recorded:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{a} \leftarrow R_z(\pi)\,\mathbf{a},
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol{\omega} \leftarrow R_z(\pi)\,\boldsymbol{\omega},
  \qquad
  q \text{ and } \mathbf{p} \text{ unchanged}.
  \label{eq:fix}
\end{equation}
\texttt{load\_trial} applies \eqref{eq:fix} in memory.
\texttt{scripts/fix\_vicon\_axes.py} writes the same sign flips into the CSV.
A file that already satisfies $c_x + c_y \ge 0$ is not rewritten.

\section{Effect of the correction}
The two tests fix the mounting from the gyroscope and the accelerometer alone.
To see what the horizontal inversion does to an attitude error, the same prefixes are given to an estimator, once with the axes as recorded and once after \eqref{eq:fix}.
The estimator is VQF, in 6-axis mode with $\tau_{\mathrm{acc}}=3$, the value fixed on BROAD and not retuned here.
It was selected because it does not depend on the filter derived in this report, so a change in its error is evidence about the axes.

The score is the inclination.
VQF has no magnetometer, so its heading differs from the optical heading by an arbitrary constant and a slow drift, and the total rotation error folds that unobservable part into the number.
Inclination does not.
The lab error quaternion is $\hat{q}\otimes q^{-1} = (w,x,y,z)$, and
\begin{equation}
  \delta = 2\arccos\sqrt{w^{2}+z^{2}}
  \label{eq:incl}
\end{equation}
is the angle between the upward lab axis and the direction $R(\hat{q}\otimes q^{-1})$ assigns to it.
A heading is a rotation about that axis, and for a pure heading $\delta = 0$.
A lab yaw $q_z(\psi)\otimes\hat{q}$ mixes $w$ with $z$ and $x$ with $y$, so it leaves $\delta$ unchanged, and removing the best constant heading offset does not alter the score.
The inversion is a body yaw $\hat{q}\otimes q_z(\pi)$.
Once the object tilts, that yaw is no longer a rotation about the lab vertical, and $\delta$ moves, which is why the heading alignment does not hide the wrong mounting.
